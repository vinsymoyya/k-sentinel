# Synthetic data card

All experimental objects are procedurally generated complex ellipse phantoms, not patient images or deidentified clinical records. No external image, annotation, trained weight or anatomy atlas is embedded in the generator. `ksentinel/core.py` documents the generator and Fourier/noise model. `results/main/splits.json` records every identity and split. The three seeds contain 1,536 training, 768 calibration, and 1,536 test objects. The same test subjects are reused across methods, challenges and budgets; **129,024 records are not 129,024 independent subjects**.

The test anatomy-shift probe changes the same procedural identity to twelve smaller ellipses rather than seven. It is paired synthetic stress, not a different hospital, ethnicity, sex, age or pathology cohort. Noise shift doubles complex-noise RMS from 0.02 to 0.04. Phantom intensity has no physical tissue unit. The simplified unitary single-coil Fourier operator omits sensitivity maps, non-Cartesian trajectories, coil noise correlations, motion, relaxation, gradient limits and many scanner effects.

The inserted and removed Gaussian templates are projected into the initial sampling operator's null space and scaled to 10% relative complex-image L2 error. Projection introduces oscillatory spatial structure; these are not clinically realistic lesions. Single-line errors are a deliberately concentrated mathematical control. Audit-aware errors know the largest audit mask and avoid it; the test is intentionally adversarial.

`example.npz` contains one representative test object and reconstructions, selected by the fixed rule first test identity in seed 11. It is not a best-case selection. `data/synthetic-demo.npz` contains the first 16 test objects from seed 11: complex truth, synthetic noisy full k-space, initial masks, audit orders, identities and noise scale. It is part of the test population, not extra training data. Full k-space is included for evaluation only; a candidate reconstruction must not see its audit or other unacquired coefficients. Complete subjects can be regenerated from `splits.json`; no large download is needed. Compressed per-record results contain only synthetic IDs and measurements of error.
